## Steelman of the Original Work (`Takehi et al., 2026-11-02`)

Takehi et al. (`arXiv:2611.00937`) formulate a creative, well-instrumented research program testing how an agentic coding model (`GPT-6 Astra`) equipped with Python tensor inspection tools interacts directly with neural network weights across increasing model complexity (`Exps 1-8`). On small multilayer perceptrons (`17.3K` to `171.7K` parameters, `Exps 1-5`) and structured synthetic circuits (`Exp 6`), the agent decodes task semantics and performs targeted weight edits from static parameter tensors alone. At transformer scale (`Gemma-3-270M` and `Qwen3.5-0.8B`, `Exps 7-8`), the authors demonstrate that giving an agent programmatic access to both weights and forward-pass diagnostics enables automated discovery and rank-1 surgical mitigation of a severe `32K`-token decoding pathology (`+40.2 pp` on `MATH-500`), and the authors transparently report all negative controls (`Exp 7a` static `0/4` and `Exp 8b` `6.8% -> 7.0%`) directly in their paper.

## Deterministic Decomposition & Witness Verification

### Witness 1: Protocol Regime Boundary Between Static Toy MLPs and `>= 270M` Transformers (`[S1]`)

Tabulating `arXiv:2611.00937`'s experimental suite by (a) whether live forward passes (`model.forward()` / `generate()`) were permitted during inspection and (b) architecture/scale separates pure static weight reading from execution-guided interpretability (`[S1]`):

| Experiment | Architecture & Nominal Parameter Scale | Forward Passes Permitted | Task Objective | Reported Result in `arXiv:2611.00937` |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| `Exps 1-5` | Toy MLPs (`17.3K` to `171.7K` params) | `0` (Static tensors only) | Toy classification & circuit edits | High accuracy from static weight inspection |
| `Exp 6` | Synthetic / bridge circuit benchmark | Static / controlled bridge | Mechanistic circuit localization | Validates small-scale weight-space primitives |
| `Exp 7a` (Static arm) | `Gemma-3-270M` Transformer (`~270M` params; `~1,572.5x` vs `Exp 5`) | `0` (Static tensors only) | Detect sycophancy fine-tune | `0 / 4` (`0.00%`; `95%` Wilson `[0.00%, 48.99%]`) |
| `Exp 7a` (Active arm) | `Gemma-3-270M` Transformer (`~270M` params) | Permitted (Active runs) | Detect sycophancy fine-tune | Succeeded once forward-pass probing was enabled |
| `Exp 8a` | `Qwen3.5-0.8B` Transformer (`~800M` params) | `25,600` token evals (`50 x 512`) | Improve `MATH-500` (`n = 500`) | `7.9% -> 48.1%` (`+40.2 pp`); `32K` loop `93.0% -> 33.1%` (`-59.9 pp`) |
| `Exp 8b` | `Gemma-3-270M` Transformer (`~270M` params) | Permitted (Active runs) | Improve `MATH-500` (`n = 500`) | `6.8% -> 7.0%` (`+0.2 pp`, `SE = 1.60 pp`, `p = 0.90`) |

Between `Exp 5` (`171.7K`-parameter MLP) and `Exp 7a` (`~270M`-parameter transformer), both model scale (`~1,572.5x` nominal ratio) and architectural indirection (multi-head attention and residual stream superposition) shift simultaneously. While `n = 4` trials in `Exp 7a` yield a wide `95%` Wilson upper bound (`48.99%`), every demonstrated success on transformer checkpoints in `arXiv:2611.00937` relied on executing forward passes on calibration inputs—placing those results in agentic activation-guided interpretability rather than zero-execution weight reading.

### Witness 2: Aggregate Loop-Abatement Accounting in `Exp 8a` and `Exp 8b` (`[S2]`, `[S3]`)

Public commentary summarizing `Exp 8a`'s `+40.2 pp` `MATH-500` increase (`7.9%` to `48.1%`) as "programming new math skill directly into weights without training" overlooks two critical protocol facts documented in `arXiv:2611.00937` (`[S2]`, `[S3]`):

1. **Forward-Pass Calibration Budget:** `GPT-6 Astra` executed `50` calibration prompts x `512` generated tokens (`25,600` forward-pass token evaluations) to diagnose why unmodified `Qwen3.5-0.8B` hit a degenerate `32K`-token repetition loop on `93.0%` of `MATH-500` items.
2. **Marginal Loop-Reduction Accounting:** Projecting out the identified rank-1 outlier direction reduced `32K` looping by `-59.9 pp` (`93.0%` down to `33.1%`) while raising `MATH-500` accuracy by `+40.2 pp` (`7.9%` to `48.1%`). As an ecological summary ratio of marginal deltas (assuming zero net degradation on the `7.0%` initially non-looping tail; paired item-level transition matrices are not required to verify the marginal bound), `+40.2 pp` corresponds to `67.11%` (`40.2 / 59.9`) of the net reduction in looping trajectories—consistent with unmasking latent pre-trained mathematical competence previously truncated by decoding repetition.
3. **Cross-Checkpoint Negative Control (`Exp 8b`):** On `Gemma-3-270M` (`n = 500` `MATH-500` items), which does not exhibit `Qwen3.5-0.8B`'s `93.0%` repetition loop, `MATH-500` accuracy moved from `6.8%` (`34/500`) to `7.0%` (`35/500`), a `+0.2 pp` difference well within two-sample binomial sampling noise (`SE = 1.60 pp`, `z = 0.12`, `p = 0.90`), though cross-architecture and baseline-floor (`6.8%`) differences mean `Exp 8b` is an informative contrast rather than an matched-architecture control (`[S3]`).

### Prospective Falsification Criteria

Two concrete empirical results would warrant revising this `OUTCOME_3` scope bound toward `OUTCOME_1`:
- **Falsification Criterion 1 (Powered Zero-Execution Transformer Audit):** A strictly zero-forward-pass agent (`model.forward()` disabled) achieving `>= 80%` accuracy across `N >= 20` blinded `>= 270M`-parameter transformer checkpoints (`80%` power against `50%` chance guessing at `alpha = 0.01`, exact binomial) paired with norm-matched clean controls.
- **Falsification Criterion 2 (Low-Rank Capability Gain Without Decoding Pathology):** Low-rank (`rank <= 8`) weight edits without gradient training producing a statistically significant (`p < 0.01`, `>= +5.0 pp` on `MATH-500` `n = 500`, `>3x` binomial `SE`) accuracy gain on a transformer checkpoint whose baseline repetition/truncation pathology rate is `<= 5.0%`.
